# 10Blob

**Share a drop. Collect an order. Continue the conversation on WhatsApp.**

10Blob is an experiment in making social commerce simpler for sellers who already run their businesses through WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, and other social channels.

Instead of asking a seller to build a full storefront, 10Blob gives them a small, focused catalog they can publish as a shareable link. A customer can browse the products, reserve available stock, place an order, and continue the conversation on WhatsApp.

## The idea

A lot of social sellers already have the audience and the products. What they often lack is a lightweight way to turn a social post or chat into a structured order without forcing the customer through a complicated ecommerce experience.

10Blob explores a simple flow:

```text
share → browse → order → WhatsApp
```

The product is intentionally small. A seller creates a drop of up to ten products, shares the link, and lets the drop do the work.

## What I built

I built the product end-to-end, including:

- seller authentication and onboarding
- product creation and image uploads
- expiring product drops
- inventory tracking and reservation
- checkout and order management
- rate limiting and abuse protection
- social preview generation
- analytics for sellers
- WhatsApp handoff after checkout

The interesting part wasn't any individual screen. It was making the small pieces behave like a real commerce system: stock needs to be reserved safely, expired drops need to stop accepting orders, and an order should leave the system in a consistent state even when something goes wrong.

## Engineering

10Blob is built with Next.js and TypeScript, with Prisma handling the data layer. Better Auth handles authentication, while UploadThing handles product media.

Upstash provides the infrastructure for Redis-backed rate limiting and related server-side controls. The application also uses Dugble for communication infrastructure.

The project has been a useful playground for thinking about the boundary between a quick product experiment and production-quality engineering.

## What I'm learning

10Blob is one of my adventures rather than a claim that I have solved social commerce.

I'm interested in what happens when you remove everything that isn't essential from a product. The constraint of a ten-product drop forces the experience to stay focused, while the commerce workflow still exposes real engineering problems around state, inventory, authentication, and reliability.

That's the part I enjoy: starting with a small idea and following it far enough that the implementation teaches me something new.
